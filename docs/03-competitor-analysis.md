# 03 — Competitor Analysis

## 1. Purpose and scope

This document positions **Shopforge** against every AI-assisted Shopify store/page-building tool surfaced in research: **Dropmagic** (the primary AI full-store-generator competitor), the established page-builder incumbents **PageFly**, **GemPages**, **Replo**, **Instant**, and **Shogun**, and Shopify's own native **Theme Editor + Shopify Magic/Sidekick** (the free baseline every paid tool competes against).

It answers three questions for planning purposes:
1. Where does Shopforge sit relative to each competitor across the dimensions that actually decide merchant trust and switching cost (compatibility risk, editor quality, pricing model, dev flexibility)?
2. Of everything competitors already do, what should Shopforge **recreate**, **improve on**, or explicitly **ignore**?
3. What does that imply for Shopforge's Must/Should/Nice/Future roadmap?

## 2. Citation key

Every factual claim about a named competitor carries the confidence tag from its source research file:

- **[VERIFIED]** — directly fetched and read from a primary/official source.
- **[SELF-REPORTED]** — the vendor's own marketing/comparison content, not independently confirmed.
- **[SEARCH-SNIPPET]** — seen only via search-result snippets, not directly fetched.
- **[REPORTED]** — from a secondary source (review site, blog, aggregator), not confirmed on the vendor's own site.
- **[INFERENCE]** — reasonable inference from adjacent verified facts.
- **[UNVERIFIED/CONFLICTING]** — sources disagree or content looked unreliable.
- **[NOT PUBLICLY VERIFIABLE]** — could not be found or confirmed at all.

Claims about **Shopforge** itself are our own product decisions (per `architecture-core.md`) and carry no citation.

Sources: `research-dropmagic.md`, `research-competitors.md` (PageFly, GemPages, Replo, Instant, Shogun, Shopify Magic/Sidekick), `architecture-core.md` (Shopforge's own architecture).

## 4. Narrative analysis

### 4.1 Dropmagic — the direct AI-full-generation competitor
Dropmagic's entire pitch is speed-to-launch for people with **no existing store**: paste a product URL, get a fully branded store in minutes [VERIFIED tagline]. That is a fundamentally different problem than Shopforge's — Dropmagic competes on "create something from nothing," Shopforge competes on "improve something that already exists and already works." The most credible independent account (buildyourstore.ai) found the marketed "2–5 minute" store actually needs "an hour or two cleaning up copy and trimming sections" before it's launch-ready [VERIFIED] — i.e., Dropmagic's real cost is not generation time, it's cleanup time, because a full-generate approach cannot know what the merchant actually wants and over-produces. Its architecture is the least transparent of anything surveyed: no source could confirm whether the published store is standard Liquid, an export, or something else [NOT PUBLICLY VERIFIABLE], and the free tier's **cannot-publish gate** is a real, well-corroborated dark pattern [SEARCH-SNIPPET, ≥4 sources]. Its CRO/post-launch story is internally contradictory across its own reviews [UNVERIFIED/CONFLICTING], which reads as an admission that Dropmagic is a one-time generator, not an ongoing tool.

### 4.2 PageFly and GemPages — the JS-overlay incumbents
Both are mature, high-volume (PageFly: 11,900+ reviews, 4.9★ [VERIFIED]), additive page builders that render through their own app-controlled JS layer rather than the theme's native rendering path. The load-bearing fact for Shopforge's positioning: **pages built with either tool stop rendering if the app is uninstalled** [REPORTED, both] — the merchant never actually owns portable theme code, they own a dependency on a subscription staying active. Neither publishes AI capable of reading the *existing* theme's structure and editing it; their AI (Smart Pages, Image-to-Layout) only generates net-new pages/sections from a prompt or image.

### 4.3 Replo — the most theme-native of the overlay builders, but still generation-first
Replo is the strongest technical benchmark among the page builders: it writes genuine Online Store 2.0 Liquid section files into the merchant's own theme, addressable by the standard `{% section %}` tag and visible in the native Theme Customizer [VERIFIED] — a materially lower compatibility-risk model than PageFly/GemPages. But it is still **generation-first**: its AI produces new pages/sections from an ad, URL, Figma frame, or brief; it does not parse the merchant's *existing* sections and propose a minimal edit to them. Files are also Replo-authored/managed (`replo-` prefixed) and depend on Replo's own republish mechanism when a merchant switches themes [VERIFIED] — closer to "theme-native" than any other overlay tool, but not the same as editing the merchant's own code in place.

### 4.4 Instant — broadest single-pass AI surface among the pure page builders
Instant combines copy, image, and layout generation in one first-draft pass — the widest AI surface of the overlay-builder group [VERIFIED] — but is explicitly positioned as a Shogun/PageFly/GemPages alternative, i.e., the same additive/overlay category, with its underlying rendering mechanism undocumented [NOT PUBLICLY VERIFIABLE].

### 4.5 Shogun — a cautionary tale on theme replacement
Shogun's core Page Builder is additive, like the others, but Shogun's history contains the single most important signal in this research set: **Shogun Frontend**, a full headless/packaged-storefront replacement product (Shopify-Hydrogen-class ambition), was announced sunset on **2024-07-12** after 9–12 months of wind-down, with Shogun explicitly citing macroeconomic pressure and merchant cost-consciousness, and pointing customers to migration partners or Shopify's own Hydrogen [VERIFIED]. A well-funded, established competitor built and then retreated from full theme/storefront replacement, back to in-theme page building. That is direct evidence that the "replace the whole storefront" model has weak retention economics relative to "work within the merchant's existing theme" — the same argument that underlies Shopforge's Principle 1 (preserve the existing theme).

### 4.6 Shopify's own Theme Editor + Magic/Sidekick — the free baseline
This is the only product surveyed that both (a) writes literally into the merchant's real theme files in a self-contained, update-safe way, and (b) via Sidekick, can locate and adjust *existing* section/block settings through natural language ("make the hero background darker") [VERIFIED block-gen; REPORTED Sidekick behavior]. It is the closest existing analogue to Shopforge's core thesis — and it's free, bundled into every non-Starter Shopify plan [VERIFIED]. Critically, though, per the research it is scoped to **adjusting values already exposed as settings** plus generating **net-new** blocks; there is no public evidence it performs deep structural analysis of a theme's actual capabilities to decide what the smallest safe edit would be, and it has no drag-drop visual editor, template library, undo/diff history, or CRO tooling to compete with the paid tools on. Any paid Shopforge tier must be a strict superset of this free baseline, or there is no reason to pay.

### 4.7 The gap: nobody does capability-aware minimal editing
Across all five commercial tools plus Shopify's own baseline, the category norm is **generate new content and place it on/in the theme** — from a prompt, image, URL, Figma file, or ad creative. None of them is built around first understanding a specific theme's actual sections/blocks/settings schema and then computing the smallest sufficient change. Replo comes closest structurally (real Liquid, in the actual theme), and Shopify Magic/Sidekick comes closest philosophically (edits real settings on request), but neither combines both: real-theme-native output *and* a capability-aware "what's the minimal edit" reasoning step *and* a proper editor/versioning/CRO feature set on top. That combination is exactly what Shopforge's Theme Manifest → Theme Model → Operation Planner (structural-ops-first, generative-ops-as-last-resort) pipeline is built to be — this is the defensible wedge this whole architecture is designed around.

## 6. Roadmap implications

### 6.1 Must-have (MVP-blocking; the core wedge)
- **Theme-capability-aware, minimal-diff editing** as the default behavior (structural ops first, generative ops only when Operation Planner confirms no existing capability satisfies the request — Principle 3). This is the gap *no* surveyed competitor fills (§4.7); it is Shopforge's reason to exist.
- **Native Liquid/Online Store 2.0 output only**, written through the same Theme Model/Serializer pipeline the visual editor uses — no JS-overlay/app-embed rendering dependency, avoiding the PageFly/GemPages "breaks on uninstall" failure mode and going one step further than Replo by editing the merchant's *own* existing sections rather than adding new Replo-style managed files.
- **Visual editor and AI sharing one ThemeModel** (Principle 7) — parity with the drag-drop quality bar set by PageFly/GemPages/Replo/Instant, but with a manual/AI consistency guarantee none of them demonstrate.
- **Cost-differentiated operations**: near-zero cost for structural ops, credits only for generative ops (Principle 9) — a real, buildable improvement over every competitor's flat page/message/upload metering.
- **Full reversibility**: Diff with `before`/`after` per entry, snapshot before destructive operations, undo (Principle 6) — directly answers Dropmagic's "one-shot generator, no post-launch tooling" weakness and the general risk of letting AI touch a merchant's live store.
- **Operation Plan preview before execution** (Principle 5) — matches and extends Sidekick's confirm-before-apply pattern, applied to both structural and generative changes.
- **Update-safe, non-forking output** — must match Shopify Magic's guarantee that generated code doesn't break the merchant's ability to update their theme; this is a trust floor, not a differentiator, but getting it wrong is disqualifying.

### 6.2 Should-have (near-term competitive parity)
- AI copywriting and image generation grounded in the real Theme Manifest/product content (parity with Dropmagic/Instant, improved via grounding).
- Branding/design-system **extraction** from the merchant's already-live theme settings (`GlobalStyles`) before any generation, improving on Replo's Brand Studio concept.
- SEO metadata generation grounded in actual page/theme content (parity with PageFly's AI Page Checkup).
- A scoped, optional product-URL import that creates a themed page/section grounded in the Manifest — not a disconnected proprietary output like Dropmagic's.
- Device preview and localization/translation, matching the baseline PageFly and Instant already ship.

### 6.3 Nice-to-have (differentiators, not launch-blocking)
- CRO/upsell suggestions and lightweight A/B testing on operations, once the core editing loop is proven — Shogun/GemPages Optimize/PageFly territory, valuable but not the wedge.
- Image-to-layout / Figma import (GemPages I2L, Replo, Instant) as an additional generation entry point.
- Chat-driven "skill shortcuts" (SEO audit, CRO analysis) in the style of Replo's assistant, built on the existing AI Gateway (doc 10).

### 6.4 Future capability (explicitly deferred)
- An optional, clearly-separated **"start from scratch" mode** for merchants with no existing store — only if it never contaminates the core "preserve the existing theme" trust promise, and only if it emits native Liquid/OS 2.0 output from day one (never a proprietary/overlay format). Directly informed by two negative signals from research: Dropmagic's full-generate output needing "an hour or two" of manual cleanup [VERIFIED], and Shogun Frontend's full-replacement product being sunset after ~1–2 years [VERIFIED]. If built, it should be framed as a bootstrapping path *into* the core editing product, not a parallel identity.
- Enterprise/agency-tier features (cross-store content sync, custom CMS collections) in the style of Shogun's top tier — relevant only once there's an installed base large enough to justify the investment.
