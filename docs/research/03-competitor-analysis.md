# 03 — Competitor Analysis

## 1. Purpose and scope

This document positions **Shopforge** against every AI-assisted Shopify store/page-building tool surfaced in research: **Dropmagic** (the primary AI full-store-generator competitor), the established page-builder incumbents **PageFly**, **GemPages**, **Replo**, **Instant**, and **Shogun**, and Shopify's own native **Theme Editor + Shopify Magic/Sidekick** (the free baseline every paid tool competes against).

It answers three questions for planning purposes:
1. Where does Shopforge sit relative to each competitor across the dimensions that actually decide merchant trust and switching cost (compatibility risk, editor quality, pricing model, dev flexibility)?
2. Of everything competitors already do, what should Shopforge **recreate**, **improve on**, or explicitly **ignore**?
3. What does that imply for Shopforge's Must/Should/Nice/Future roadmap?

## 2. Citation key

Every factual claim about a named competitor carries the confidence tag from its source research file:

- **[VERIFIED]** — directly fetched and read from a primary/official source.
- **[SELF-REPORTED]** — the vendor's own marketing/comparison content, not independently confirmed.
- **[SEARCH-SNIPPET]** — seen only via search-result snippets, not directly fetched.
- **[REPORTED]** — from a secondary source (review site, blog, aggregator), not confirmed on the vendor's own site.
- **[INFERENCE]** — reasonable inference from adjacent verified facts.
- **[UNVERIFIED/CONFLICTING]** — sources disagree or content looked unreliable.
- **[NOT PUBLICLY VERIFIABLE]** — could not be found or confirmed at all.

Claims about **Shopforge** itself are our own product decisions and carry no citation, consistent with doc 01 §0's convention.

Sources: `research-dropmagic.md`, `research-competitors.md` (PageFly, GemPages, Replo, Instant, Shogun, Shopify Magic/Sidekick). Claims about Shopforge's own architecture reflect the current product direction described in doc 01 §4, not a separate architecture document.

## 4. Narrative analysis

### 4.1 Dropmagic — the direct AI-full-generation competitor
Dropmagic's entire pitch is speed-to-launch for people with **no existing store**: paste a product URL, get a fully branded store in minutes [VERIFIED tagline]. That is a fundamentally different problem than Shopforge's — Dropmagic competes on "create something from nothing," Shopforge competes on "improve something that already exists and already works." The most credible independent account (buildyourstore.ai) found the marketed "2–5 minute" store actually needs "an hour or two cleaning up copy and trimming sections" before it's launch-ready [VERIFIED] — i.e., Dropmagic's real cost is not generation time, it's cleanup time, because a full-generate approach cannot know what the merchant actually wants and over-produces. Its architecture is the least transparent of anything surveyed: no source could confirm whether the published store is standard Liquid, an export, or something else [NOT PUBLICLY VERIFIABLE], and the free tier's **cannot-publish gate** is a real, well-corroborated dark pattern [SEARCH-SNIPPET, ≥4 sources]. Its CRO/post-launch story is internally contradictory across its own reviews [UNVERIFIED/CONFLICTING], which reads as an admission that Dropmagic is a one-time generator, not an ongoing tool.

### 4.2 PageFly and GemPages — the JS-overlay incumbents
Both are mature, high-volume (PageFly: 11,900+ reviews, 4.9★ [VERIFIED]), additive page builders that render through their own app-controlled JS layer rather than the theme's native rendering path. The load-bearing fact for Shopforge's positioning: **pages built with either tool stop rendering if the app is uninstalled** [REPORTED, both] — the merchant never actually owns portable theme code, they own a dependency on a subscription staying active. Neither publishes AI capable of reading the *existing* theme's structure and editing it; their AI (Smart Pages, Image-to-Layout) only generates net-new pages/sections from a prompt or image.

### 4.3 Replo — the most theme-native of the overlay builders, but still generation-first
Replo is the strongest technical benchmark among the page builders: it writes genuine Online Store 2.0 Liquid section files into the merchant's own theme, addressable by the standard `{% section %}` tag and visible in the native Theme Customizer [VERIFIED] — a materially lower compatibility-risk model than PageFly/GemPages. But it is still **generation-first**: its AI produces new pages/sections from an ad, URL, Figma frame, or brief; it does not parse the merchant's *existing* sections and propose a minimal edit to them. Files are also Replo-authored/managed (`replo-` prefixed) and depend on Replo's own republish mechanism when a merchant switches themes [VERIFIED] — closer to "theme-native" than any other overlay tool, but not the same as editing the merchant's own code in place.

### 4.4 Instant — broadest single-pass AI surface among the pure page builders
Instant combines copy, image, and layout generation in one first-draft pass — the widest AI surface of the overlay-builder group [VERIFIED] — but is explicitly positioned as a Shogun/PageFly/GemPages alternative, i.e., the same additive/overlay category, with its underlying rendering mechanism undocumented [NOT PUBLICLY VERIFIABLE].

### 4.5 Shogun — a cautionary tale on theme replacement
Shogun's core Page Builder is additive, like the others, but Shogun's history contains the single most important signal in this research set: **Shogun Frontend**, a full headless/packaged-storefront replacement product (Shopify-Hydrogen-class ambition), was announced sunset on **2024-07-12** after 9–12 months of wind-down, with Shogun explicitly citing macroeconomic pressure and merchant cost-consciousness, and pointing customers to migration partners or Shopify's own Hydrogen [VERIFIED]. A well-funded, established competitor built and then retreated from full theme/storefront replacement, back to in-theme page building. That is direct evidence that the "replace the whole storefront with a custom/headless runtime" model has weak retention economics relative to a native, standard-Liquid-theme approach — relevant to Shopforge's own choice to publish onto a normal Base Theme via the Shopify Admin API rather than a headless storefront, even though Shopforge's store-generation flow is otherwise now generation-first, much closer to Dropmagic's than to a pure "edit the merchant's existing theme" model (see doc 01 §4).

### 4.6 Shopify's own Theme Editor + Magic/Sidekick — the free baseline
This is the only product surveyed that both (a) writes literally into the merchant's real theme files in a self-contained, update-safe way, and (b) via Sidekick, can locate and adjust *existing* section/block settings through natural language ("make the hero background darker") [VERIFIED block-gen; REPORTED Sidekick behavior]. It is the closest existing analogue to what Shopforge's core architecture used to be built around (see doc 01 §4 for the current architecture and differentiator) — and it's free, bundled into every non-Starter Shopify plan [VERIFIED]. Critically, though, per the research it is scoped to **adjusting values already exposed as settings** plus generating **net-new** blocks; there is no public evidence it performs deep structural analysis of a theme's actual capabilities to decide what the smallest safe edit would be, and it has no drag-drop visual editor, template library, undo/diff history, or CRO tooling to compete with the paid tools on. Any paid Shopforge tier must be a strict superset of this free baseline, or there is no reason to pay.

### 4.7 The gap: nobody does capability-aware minimal editing
Across all five commercial tools plus Shopify's own baseline, the category norm is **generate new content and place it on/in the theme** — from a prompt, image, URL, Figma file, or ad creative. None of them is built around first understanding a specific theme's actual sections/blocks/settings schema and then computing the smallest sufficient change. Replo comes closest structurally (real Liquid, in the actual theme), and Shopify Magic/Sidekick comes closest philosophically (edits real settings on request), but neither combines both: real-theme-native output *and* a capability-aware "what's the minimal edit" reasoning step *and* a proper editor/versioning/CRO feature set on top.

That finding is still accurate as competitor research, and it remains a genuine, unclaimed opportunity in the category — see §6.4 for it as a possible future direction. But it is no longer the gap Shopforge's own architecture is built to exploit. Shopforge has pivoted to a generation-first model much closer to Dropmagic's than to the "parse an unknown theme and minimally edit it" approach this section describes: a fixed, first-party section library, AI-driven `Store Configuration` generation, a LiquidJS preview that renders the real production Liquid, and publish onto a Base Theme we own. For the reasoning behind what now differentiates Shopforge given that pivot, see doc 01 §4.

## 6. Roadmap implications

### 6.1 Must-have (MVP-blocking; the core wedge)
- **A fixed, first-party-owned, quality-controlled section library** (roughly 40–60 sections), each a real Liquid template + Liquid schema + editor metadata + shared settings contract, authored and audited before any merchant ever uses it. This is what stands in for the old "capability-aware editing" wedge — see §4.7 and doc 01 §4 for why: the differentiation now comes from owning and controlling the entire section library and rendering pipeline, not from parsing an arbitrary merchant theme.
- **Product Import/Scraper**: a product URL is the primary input; scraped product data (title, images, specs, reviews) drives AI section selection, ordering, and settings/content generation.
- **A `Store Configuration` JSON document** (`pages → sections[] → {id, type, settings, blocks}`) as the single source of truth read and written by the AI, the visual editor, and the preview renderer alike.
- **A LiquidJS Preview Renderer** that loads each section's actual production Liquid template and schema, injects `Store Configuration` settings, and renders it into a same-origin preview iframe — so what the merchant previews during editing is the same Liquid that ships to Shopify, not a React approximation of it (doc 01 §4). This is the single biggest differentiator this research surfaced no competitor as documented offering.
- **AI generation scoped to structured operations only** — section selection, ordering, settings, and content against the `Store Configuration` — never Liquid, HTML, CSS, or JS generation.
- **Visual editor (React/Next.js) built on the same `Store Configuration` the AI mutates**, so manual edits and AI edits stay consistent by construction and are reflected immediately in the LiquidJS preview — parity with the drag-drop quality bar set by PageFly/GemPages/Replo/Instant, but with a manual/AI consistency guarantee none of them demonstrate.
- **A Base Theme we own and control** (layout/, sections/, snippets/, assets/, config/, templates/, locales/) containing the section library plus required theme infrastructure, installable into a merchant's Shopify store.
- **Publishing via the Shopify Admin API** that applies the `Store Configuration` (section order, settings, content, product references, assets, templates) onto the merchant's installed Base Theme — real, native Liquid/Online Store 2.0 output, no JS-overlay/app-embed rendering dependency for the live storefront, avoiding the PageFly/GemPages "breaks on uninstall" failure mode (§4.2).

### 6.2 Should-have (near-term competitive parity)
- AI copywriting and image generation grounded in the actual scraped product data and each section's real content schema (parity with Dropmagic/Instant, improved via grounding).
- A consistent, Base Theme-level `GlobalStyles` design system (colors, typography, buttons, spacing) applied across every AI-generated section — in the spirit of Replo's Brand Studio concept, but set once at the Base Theme/Store Configuration level rather than extracted from a merchant's pre-existing theme, since there isn't one in this flow.
- SEO metadata generation grounded in the actual product data and generated section content (parity with PageFly's AI Page Checkup).
- Device preview and localization/translation, matching the baseline PageFly and Instant already ship.

### 6.3 Nice-to-have (differentiators, not launch-blocking)
- CRO/upsell suggestions and lightweight A/B testing on operations, once the core generation-and-publish loop is proven — Shogun/GemPages Optimize/PageFly territory, valuable but not the wedge.
- Image-to-layout / Figma import (GemPages I2L, Replo, Instant) as an additional generation entry point.
- Chat-driven "skill shortcuts" (SEO audit, CRO analysis) in the style of Replo's assistant, built on the existing AI Gateway (doc 10).

### 6.4 Future capability (explicitly deferred)
- **Importing and editing an arbitrary existing merchant theme in place** — the old core architecture (Theme Manifest/Theme Model, capability-aware minimal-diff editing) this pivot moved away from (doc 01 §4). Genuinely interesting as a future direction once the section-library/Store Configuration/LiquidJS pipeline is proven: "parse an existing theme's real capabilities and make the smallest sufficient AI edit" is still a gap no researched competitor fills (§4.7), and remains available as a longer-term differentiator. But it is a materially larger engineering problem than the current MVP — arbitrary theme parsing, capability detection across unknown section/schema shapes, and validation of AI-generated Liquid against a theme we don't control — and none of it is required for, or should be blended into, the current section-library generation flow. If pursued, it should be a clearly separate mode with its own validation story, not a re-merger with the MVP architecture.
- Enterprise/agency-tier features (cross-store content sync, custom CMS collections) in the style of Shogun's top tier — relevant only once there's an installed base large enough to justify the investment.
